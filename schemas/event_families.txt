TASK

Identify all meaningful event statements expressed in the SEC 10-K text
and classify each event into one event family.

A text chunk may contain:

- zero events
- one event
- multiple events
- multiple different event families

Identify semantically distinct events separately.

Do NOT perform full N-ary extraction in this step.

Do NOT extract:

- subject
- subject_type
- relation
- object
- object_type
- additional semantic roles

Those tasks are handled by later nodes.


1. EVENT FAMILIES


Competition

A Competition event describes an actual competitive relationship,
competitor identity, competitive comparison, competitive pressure,
or competitive advantage or disadvantage relative to another
market participant, competing product, or competing offering.

Examples:

"Our primary competitors include Company A and Company B."

→ Competition


"Our technology has significant performance advantages over
competing products."

→ Competition


"We face increasing competition from Chinese manufacturers."

→ Competition


Do NOT classify a sentence as Competition merely because words such as:

competition
competitive
competitor

appear.


A generic market-position statement such as:

"we are a global leader"
"we are a leading supplier"
"we hold a strong market position"

does NOT by itself establish a Competition event.


Customer relationships, supplier relationships, partnerships,
market presence, or broad industry leadership are not Competition
unless the source explicitly describes a competitive relationship,
comparison, pressure, advantage, disadvantage, or competitor.


Example:

"Adient maintains longstanding relationships with major OEMs."

→ OTHER or no event


Example:

"Adient is a global leader in automotive seating."

→ OTHER or no event


------------------------------------------------------------


Production

A Production event describes production activity when the
main semantic focus is production OUTPUT, VOLUME, CAPACITY,
SCALE, STATUS, LEVEL, CHANGE, SHARE, or TIMING.

Production is OUTPUT-CENTERED.

Typical semantic questions answered by a Production event include:

- What is produced?
- How much is produced?
- What production capacity exists or is planned?
- Where is it produced?
- When does production begin?
- Did production increase or decrease?
- What production level was reached?
- What share of production is represented?

Examples:

"Approximately 93 million vehicles were produced in 2023."

→ Production


"The plant produced 500,000 tons of copper in 2024."

→ Production


"Steel production in Japan decreased."

→ Production


"The facility began commercial production."

→ Production


"The plant has annual production capacity of 150,000 units."

→ Production


A reporting attribution does not change the event family.

Example:

"According to Association X, approximately 93 million vehicles
were produced in 2023."

→ Production

Association X is the reporting source.
The underlying event is still Production.


Do NOT classify a statement as Production merely because the words:

production
produce
producer
capacity

appear.


A hypothetical adverse consequence is NOT a Production event.

Example:

"A supplier shortage could halt production."

→ OTHER

The production halt has not occurred.


A planned or expected production action may still be Production.

Example:

"We plan to begin production in 2027."

→ Production


------------------------------------------------------------


Supply

A Supply event describes the actual FLOW, DELIVERY, SHIPMENT,
DISTRIBUTION, or PROVISION of a PHYSICAL product, component,
material, commodity, or other good from one entity to another party.

The central meaning is that physical goods move or are provided
to a customer, recipient, destination, or downstream party.

Examples:

"We deliver battery modules to our customers."

→ Supply


"We supply seat systems to automotive manufacturers."

→ Supply


"We ship components to assembly plants."

→ Supply


Supply is different from Service_Provision.

Compare:

"We deliver physical battery modules to customers."

→ Supply


"We provide logistics services to customers."

→ Service_Provision


A statement describing only selling, marketing,
commercialization, pricing, revenue, or customer demand
is NOT Supply.

Example:

"We sell vehicles to customers."

→ OTHER

when Sales is not supported in the current experiment.


Do NOT classify expressions such as:

"supply chain solutions"
"supply chain management"
"supply chain planning"

as Supply unless the text actually describes physical goods
being supplied, delivered, shipped, or distributed.


A hypothetical inability to deliver goods is not a realized
Supply event.

Example:

"A natural disaster may prevent us from delivering products."

→ OTHER


------------------------------------------------------------


Acquisition

An Acquisition event describes a transaction in which an entity
acquires, purchases, exchanges for, takes control of, or otherwise
obtains another company, business, segment, facility, major asset,
or ownership interest.

The central meaning is the acquisition transaction itself.

Examples:

"We completed the acquisition of Stelco."

→ Acquisition


"We acquired the automotive business of Company A."

→ Acquisition


"We plan to acquire Company B."

→ Acquisition


Do NOT classify ordinary procurement or purchasing of materials,
components, inventory, products, or services for normal operations
as Acquisition.


Do NOT classify generic or static ownership statements as Acquisition.

Example:

"We own 50% of Joint Venture A."

→ OTHER

when Equity_Ownership is not supported in the current experiment.


Do NOT classify a trade-in purchase or routine purchase of a product
as Acquisition merely because the verb "acquire" or "purchase" appears.


A strategic, operational, financial, or market effect caused by
an acquisition is not automatically another Acquisition event.

Classify the effect according to its own semantic meaning.

If the appropriate family is not supported in the current experiment,
classify the effect as OTHER.


------------------------------------------------------------


Product_Portfolio

A Product_Portfolio event describes WHAT products, product lines,
product types, commodities, or commercial offerings are included
in a company's or segment's current or expected portfolio.

The central meaning is portfolio COMPOSITION rather than
production activity, technology use, or product development.

Examples:

"Our product portfolio includes Product A and Product B."

→ Product_Portfolio


"Our major products include copper, molybdenum, zinc and silver."

→ Product_Portfolio


"The segment offers axles and driveshafts."

→ Product_Portfolio


Compare:

"Our products include copper and zinc."

→ Product_Portfolio


"We produced 500,000 tons of copper."

→ Production


"We refine copper ore into concentrate."

→ Manufacturing


"We use artificial intelligence in our operations."

→ OTHER

when Technology is not supported in the current experiment.


"We are developing a new battery platform."

→ Development


Do NOT classify a portfolio statement as Production merely
because words such as "produce" or "production" appear.


Do NOT classify raw-material use as Product_Portfolio.

Example:

"We use steel and aluminum in production."

→ OTHER

when Raw_Material_Use is not supported in the current experiment.


------------------------------------------------------------


Service_Provision

A Service_Provision event describes the actual provision,
delivery, performance, maintenance, installation, support,
repair, or access to a NON-PHYSICAL service or service activity
for a customer, user, recipient, or other external party.

Services may include:

- technical services
- procurement support
- warehousing services
- logistics services
- maintenance services
- repair services
- installation services
- customer support
- access to a service network
- other non-physical service activities

Examples:

"We provide logistics services to customers."

→ Service_Provision


"We provide maintenance and repair services."

→ Service_Provision


"Customers receive access to the charging network."

→ Service_Provision


Service_Provision is different from Supply.

Physical goods provided to another party:

→ Supply

Non-physical services or support provided to another party:

→ Service_Provision


Internal workforce activities are NOT Service_Provision.

Examples:

"We provide employee training."

→ OTHER


"We offer leadership development programs to employees."

→ OTHER

when Workforce is not supported in the current experiment.


Do NOT classify generic product benefits or capabilities as
Service_Provision merely because the verb "provide" appears.


------------------------------------------------------------


Manufacturing

A Manufacturing event describes an actual manufacturing,
processing, fabrication, assembly, refining, milling, smelting,
or other physical transformation process.

Manufacturing is PROCESS-CENTERED.

The central meaning concerns HOW a material, component, or
product is made, processed, transformed, assembled, refined,
or fabricated.

Examples:

"The company manufactures semiconductor devices."

→ Manufacturing


"The operation mills and floats copper ore."

→ Manufacturing


"The facility processes ore into concentrate."

→ Manufacturing


"The products are fabricated using third-party wafer fabs."

→ Manufacturing


Manufacturing is different from Production.

Compare:

"The plant produced 500,000 tons of concentrate."

→ Production

because the focus is output and volume.


"The plant processed ore into concentrate."

→ Manufacturing

because the focus is transformation and process.


Testing performed as part of research, engineering validation,
product development, or technology development belongs to Development.

Example:

"We test new battery designs."

→ Development


Testing may be Manufacturing only when the source explicitly
describes testing as part of a routine manufacturing,
production, or quality-control process.


General production statistics are normally Production,
not Manufacturing.

Do NOT duplicate the same proposition as both Production and
Manufacturing merely because both interpretations are possible.

Use the main semantic focus of the proposition.


A hypothetical inability to manufacture is not a realized
Manufacturing event.

Example:

"A natural disaster could prevent us from manufacturing products."

→ OTHER


------------------------------------------------------------


Development

A Development event describes research, creation, design,
engineering, testing, development, advancement, or improvement
of a product, technology, system, solution, method, or
technical capability.

Development focuses on CREATION or ADVANCEMENT rather than
routine production, manufacturing, technology use, or
facility construction.

Examples:

"The company develops inert anode technology."

→ Development


"The partnership advances development of a new smelting technology."

→ Development


"We design a new semiconductor technology."

→ Development


"We test new battery designs."

→ Development


Compare:

"We manufacture semiconductor devices."

→ Manufacturing


"We produced 10,000 semiconductor devices."

→ Production


"We use artificial intelligence in manufacturing."

→ OTHER

when Technology is not supported in the current experiment.


Do NOT classify routine manufacturing as Development.

Do NOT classify production volume as Development.

Do NOT classify simple use, adoption, deployment, or integration
of an existing technology as Development unless the source also
states that the company develops, designs, engineers, tests,
creates, or improves that technology.


Building, opening, expanding, converting, upgrading, or relocating
a physical facility is NOT Development.

Example:

"The company will design and build a processing facility."

→ OTHER

when the central event is Facility_Development and that family
is not currently supported.


Do not classify establishment of a company, joint venture,
committee, council, office, or other organization as Development.


------------------------------------------------------------

does not belong to any currently supported family:

Competition
Production
Supply
Acquisition
Product_Portfolio
Service_Provision
Manufacturing
Development


Examples of meaningful events that may currently be OTHER include:

- Procurement
- Raw_Material_Use
- Technology
- Product_Launch
- Discontinuation
- Facility_Development
- Investment
- Collaboration
- Divestiture
- Agreement
- Financing
- Corporate_Formation
- Corporate_Restructuring
- Regulatory_Event
- Workforce
- Operational_Impact
- Risk
- Seasonality
- Equity_Ownership
- Litigation
- Recall
- Cybersecurity_Incident
- Strategy_Execution
- Strategy_Change
- other unsupported meaningful event types


Do NOT force an unsupported event into the closest supported family.


Example:

"The company was awarded a contract to build a facility."

may contain meaningful Agreement and Facility_Development events.

→ OTHER


Example:

"We use lithium in battery production."

→ OTHER

when Raw_Material_Use is not supported.


Example:

"We deployed artificial intelligence in our operations."

→ OTHER

when Technology is not supported.


OTHER is used only for meaningful events.

Do NOT use OTHER merely to preserve every descriptive statement.


Purely static, descriptive, background, identity, headquarters,
location, organizational, market-position, or market-description
facts may produce no event when they do not express meaningful
event semantics.


Examples:

"Our headquarters are in Ohio."

→ no event


"We operate in 30 countries."

→ no event

when the statement is only a static geographic description.


"We are a leading supplier."

→ no event

when the statement only describes market position.


2. IMPORTANT FAMILY BOUNDARIES

Use the COMPLETE SEMANTIC MEANING of the proposition.

Do NOT classify only from a keyword or verb.


PRODUCTION vs MANUFACTURING

Production:

focuses on output, volume, capacity, production level,
production status, production timing, production change,
or production share.

Manufacturing:

focuses on manufacturing method, processing, fabrication,
assembly, refining, or physical transformation.


Example:

"93 million vehicles were produced."

→ Production


"Vehicles are assembled at the facility."

→ Manufacturing


------------------------------------------------------------


PRODUCTION vs PRODUCT_PORTFOLIO

Production:

reports actual production activity, capacity, status, level,
output, volume, timing, or change.

Product_Portfolio:

identifies what products, product lines, product types,
commodities, or commercial offerings belong to the company's
or segment's portfolio.


Example:

"The company produced 10 million pounds of copper."

→ Production


"The company's major products include copper and zinc."

→ Product_Portfolio


------------------------------------------------------------


SUPPLY vs SERVICE_PROVISION

Supply:

physical goods flow to another party.

Service_Provision:

non-physical service or support is provided to another party.


Example:

"We deliver electronic controllers to customers."

→ Supply


"We provide logistics services to customers."

→ Service_Provision


------------------------------------------------------------


SUPPLY vs SALES

Supply:

physical delivery, shipment, distribution, or supply
of goods to another party.

Sales:

selling, marketing, commercialization, pricing,
revenue, or customer purchasing activity.


Example:

"We ship seats to customers."

→ Supply


"We sell seats to customers."

→ OTHER

when Sales is not currently supported.


------------------------------------------------------------


DEVELOPMENT vs MANUFACTURING

Development:

research, creation, design, engineering, testing,
advancement, or improvement of a product, technology,
system, method, or capability.

Manufacturing:

actual fabrication, processing, assembly, refining,
or physical manufacturing activity.


Example:

"We design intelligent power technology."

→ Development


"We manufacture intelligent power devices."

→ Manufacturing


------------------------------------------------------------


DEVELOPMENT vs TECHNOLOGY

Development:

the company creates, designs, engineers, tests,
develops, advances, or improves a technology.

Technology:

the company uses, adopts, deploys, integrates,
or operates an existing technology.


Example:

"We develop an AI-based control system."

→ Development


"We use AI in our manufacturing operations."

→ OTHER

when Technology is not currently supported.


------------------------------------------------------------


DEVELOPMENT vs FACILITY DEVELOPMENT

Developing a technology, product, system, method,
or technical capability:

→ Development


Building, opening, expanding, converting, upgrading,
or relocating a physical factory, mine, processing plant,
research center, or other facility:

→ OTHER

when Facility_Development is not currently supported.


------------------------------------------------------------


ACQUISITION vs PROCUREMENT

Acquisition:

obtaining ownership or control of a company,
business, segment, facility, major asset,
or ownership interest.

Procurement:

obtaining materials, components, products,
inventory, or services for ordinary operations.


Example:

"We acquired Company A."

→ Acquisition


"We purchased aluminum from suppliers."

→ OTHER

when Procurement is not currently supported.


------------------------------------------------------------


ACQUISITION vs EQUITY OWNERSHIP

An acquisition transaction that obtains an ownership interest:

→ Acquisition


A static statement describing an ownership position:

→ OTHER

when Equity_Ownership is not currently supported.


Example:

"We acquired a 40% interest in Company A."

→ Acquisition


"We own 40% of Company A."

→ OTHER


------------------------------------------------------------


PRODUCT_PORTFOLIO vs RAW MATERIAL USE

Product_Portfolio:

describes products or commercial offerings belonging
to the company or segment portfolio.

Raw_Material_Use:

describes materials used, required, consumed,
or relied upon in production or operations.


Example:

"Our products include battery cells."

→ Product_Portfolio


"We use lithium to manufacture battery cells."

→ OTHER

when Raw_Material_Use is not currently supported.


3. HYPOTHETICAL, RISK, AND NEGATED STATEMENTS

A hypothetical adverse consequence must NOT be classified
as a supported realized event merely because it mentions
production, supply, manufacturing, development, acquisition,
or another supported family.


Statements containing expressions such as:

may
could
might
if
potentially
possible
would

require careful interpretation.


Example:

"A supplier shortage could halt production."

→ OTHER

The production halt has not occurred.
The underlying event is a Risk event, which is not supported
in the current experiment.


Example:

"A natural disaster may prevent us from delivering products."

→ OTHER

Do NOT classify this as Supply.


Example:

"Component shortages caused production shutdowns."

The adverse impact actually occurred.

Do not treat the shutdown as hypothetical.


Planned or expected actions are different from hypothetical risks.

Example:

"We plan to begin production in 2027."

→ Production

because beginning production is an explicitly intended action.


Example:

"We expect to acquire Company A next year."

→ Acquisition


Do NOT convert explicitly negated statements into positive events.


Example:

"We have not experienced material shortages."

Do NOT extract a shortage event.


Example:

"We did not acquire Company A."

Do NOT classify this as Acquisition.


4. MULTIPLE EVENTS

1. Identify every SEMANTICALLY DISTINCT meaningful event.

2. One sentence may contain multiple event families.

Example:

"We design, manufacture, and deliver intelligent power products
to customers."

contains three meaningful events:

- Development
- Manufacturing
- Supply


3. Do not merge distinct actions simply because they occur in the
same sentence or paragraph.


4. A coordinated list of multiple objects does NOT need to be split
into separate object-level candidates in this classification step.

A later Atomic Splitter node is responsible for splitting lists.

Example:

"Our competitors include Company A, Company B, and Company C."

At this stage, one broad Competition candidate is sufficient.

The later Atomic Splitter will create separate candidates for:

Company A
Company B
Company C


5. The same applies to coordinated:

- products
- competitors
- materials
- services
- customers
- recipients
- locations
- acquisition targets

Do not spend this classification step performing detailed
object-level splitting.


5. CLASSIFICATION RULES

1. Identify all semantically distinct meaningful events.

2. Classify each event according to its complete semantic meaning.

3. Do not classify from one keyword or verb alone.

4. Use surrounding context when necessary.

5. Do not force an event into a supported family.

6. Use OTHER only when a meaningful event belongs to an unsupported
   event type.

7. Purely static or descriptive statements may produce no event.

8. Do not invent events.

9. Every candidate must be traceable to explicit source text.

10. Reporting sources, introductory phrases, or contextual entities
    should not automatically become event actors.

11. Filing-company context does not mean that every event is
    performed by the filing company.

12. If one source sentence explicitly expresses several independent
    actions, preserve those actions as separate event candidates.

13. Do not duplicate one proposition into multiple families merely
    because several families are superficially plausible.

Choose the family that best represents the proposition's main
semantic meaning.

14. Do not convert hypothetical adverse consequences into realized
    supported events.

15. Do not convert negated statements into positive events.

Return exactly ONE valid JSON object in the following format:

{
  "events": [
    {
      "event_family": "Competition",
      "evidence": "exact or concise supporting text span"
    },
    {
      "event_family": "Manufacturing",
      "evidence": "exact or concise supporting text span"
    }
  ]
}


Allowed event_family values are ONLY:

Competition
Production
Supply
Acquisition
Product_Portfolio
Service_Provision
Manufacturing
Development
OTHER


The "evidence" field should contain the exact or concise
source-supported statement that most directly supports the event.

Do NOT return:

- event_type beyond event_family
- subject
- subject_type
- relation
- object
- object_type
- additional semantic roles

Those are extracted by later nodes.


If no meaningful event is present, return:

{
  "events": []
}


Return JSON ONLY.
