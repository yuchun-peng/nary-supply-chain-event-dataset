TASK

Extract structured N-ary events from natural-language SEC 10-K text.

Each extracted event must be explicit, meaningful, and traceable
to the source text.

Event-specific definitions and role requirements are provided
separately by the corresponding event schema.


1. CORE N-ARY STRUCTURE

Each event represents one main relation:

subject + relation + object

Every event MUST contain:

event_type
subject
subject_type
relation
object
object_type

Additional semantic roles may be included when explicitly
supported by the source text.


2. GENERAL ANNOTATION RULES

1. Each N-ary event should represent ONE main piece of information.

2. relation should preserve the main verb or verb phrase expressed
   in the source text as closely as possible.

3. event_type represents the broader semantic meaning of the event.

4. Determine event_type from the complete event meaning, including:

   - subject
   - relation
   - object
   - additional semantic roles
   - surrounding textual context

5. Do NOT determine event_type from verb matching alone.

6. Different relations may belong to the same event type when they
   express the same underlying semantic event.

7. Every extracted fact must be supported by the source text.

8. Do not invent unsupported entities, relations, roles, values,
   causes, purposes, agents, or other information.

9. Information may be normalized only when it is explicitly and
   unambiguously resolvable from the source text.

10. Do not replace an explicitly stated source relation with a more
    typical relation merely because it is common for the event type.

11. Passive voice must preserve the correct semantic direction.
    Do not invent an unstated agent.

12. Extract explicit and meaningful events rather than omitting
    them unnecessarily.


3. EVENT SEPARATION

1. If a sentence contains multiple independent actions,
   extract them as separate N-ary events.

Example:

"We design, build and sell vehicles."

should normally produce separate events for:

design
build
sell


2. If the same relation applies to multiple independent objects,
   create a separate event for each object.

Example:

"A supplies products C and D."

should normally produce:

A supplies C
A supplies D


3. Do not combine multiple independent products, materials,
   companies, technologies, facilities, or other entities into
   one object when each can independently form an event.

4. One sentence or text chunk may therefore contain zero, one,
   or multiple N-ary events.


4. REFERENCE RESOLUTION

When the filing company is explicitly identifiable, references such as:

we
our
us
ourselves
the Company

should be normalized to the filing company's full name when they
clearly refer to the filing company.

ORG is document-relative.

Only the SEC filing company itself may be labeled ORG.

Do not automatically assign the filing company as the subject.

Official operating segments or formally disclosed business units
must be labeled SEGMENT.

External companies, subsidiaries, joint ventures, suppliers,
customers, government agencies, regulators, unions and other
formal organizations must be labeled COMP.

Do not resolve a reference when its antecedent is ambiguous.

Do not invent an entity name that cannot be determined from
the source text or supplied context.


5. ENTITY TYPES

Use ONLY the following entity types.


ORG

Only the SEC filing company itself.

Examples:

General Motors Company in a GM filing
Ford Motor Company in a Ford filing
Lear Corporation in a Lear filing


------------------------------------------------------------

COMP

External formal organizations appearing in the filing.

This includes external companies, subsidiaries, joint ventures,
competitors, suppliers, customers, partners, government agencies,
regulators, unions, associations and other formal organizations.

Examples:

EVgo
Panasonic
European Commission
United Auto Workers


------------------------------------------------------------

PERSON

Named people or explicitly identified individual positions.

Examples:

Mary Barra
Chief Executive Officer


------------------------------------------------------------

PRODUCT

Physical or commercial products, components, vehicles,
equipment or product offerings.

Examples:

vehicles
seat systems
battery cells
axles
controllers


------------------------------------------------------------

TECHNOLOGY

Software, technical systems, platforms, AI capabilities,
algorithms, technical architectures, digital systems
and other technological capabilities.

Examples:

artificial intelligence
vehicle software
autonomous driving system
battery management software
IT security system


------------------------------------------------------------

MATERIAL

Raw materials, energy resources, chemicals, commodities,
minerals or natural resources used in production or operations.

Examples:

steel
aluminum
lithium
rubber
electricity


------------------------------------------------------------

FACILITY

Factories, manufacturing plants, research centers,
warehouses, offices, charging infrastructure,
or other physical facilities and infrastructure.

Examples:

AMP-1
Normal plant
development center
charging network


------------------------------------------------------------

LOCATION

Countries, regions, cities, states, geographic areas,
or geographic markets.

Examples:

China
Europe
United States
Ohio


------------------------------------------------------------

SEGMENT

Official operating segments, divisions, business units,
or formally disclosed organizational segments.

Examples:

GM North America
GM International
Seating
E-Systems


------------------------------------------------------------

REGULATION

Laws, regulations, formal rules, standards, permits,
certifications and formal regulatory requirements.

Examples:

GDPR
Inflation Reduction Act
emissions regulations
ISO standards


------------------------------------------------------------

AGREEMENT

Contracts, warranties, leases, supply agreements,
credit agreements, guarantees, licenses and other
formal arrangements.

Examples:

supply agreement
warranty
lease agreement
credit facility


------------------------------------------------------------

IMPACT_EVENT

An actual or potential situation, disruption, risk source,
or adverse event affecting operations, supply chains,
production, sales, costs or markets.

Examples:

semiconductor shortage
labor strike
cyberattack
natural disaster


------------------------------------------------------------

METRIC_VALUE

Amounts, quantities, percentages, capacities,
financial values, operational measurements,
or other quantitative values.

Examples:

$188 million
15%
39,600 employees
150,000 units per year


------------------------------------------------------------

TIME

Explicit dates, years, periods, quarters,
deadlines or other temporal expressions.

Examples:

2024
December 31, 2024
third quarter
2030


------------------------------------------------------------

OTHER

Entities that do not fit any other allowed entity type.

Use OTHER rather than forcing an entity into an
incorrect type.


6. EVENT TYPE SELECTION

The event-type schema is CLOSED.

Use ONLY the event types explicitly supported
by the current experiment.

Do NOT create new event types.

Determine the event type from the semantic meaning
of the complete event rather than relation wording alone.

Do not force an unsupported event into the closest
supported event family.

If a meaningful event does not fit any supported
event type, classify it as OTHER at the classification stage.


7. MODAL, FUTURE AND REALIZED EVENTS

Distinguish carefully between realized events and
hypothetical or future consequences.

Statements containing:

may
could
might
if
potentially
possible
would

must NOT automatically be treated as realized events.

Example:

"A supplier shortage could halt production."

The production halt has not occurred.

Do NOT extract it as a realized Production event.


Example:

"Supplier shortages caused production shutdowns."

The impact actually occurred.


Planned or expected actions may still be extracted
when the action itself is explicitly intended.

Example:

"We plan to begin production in 2027."

This may be extracted as a Production event with:

event_status = planned


8. NEGATION

Do NOT convert an explicitly negated statement
into a positive event.

Example:

"We have not experienced material shortages."

Do NOT extract a shortage event.


Example:

"No legal action has been initiated."

Do NOT extract a litigation event.


9. ADDITIONAL N-ARY ROLES

In addition to the six required core fields:

event_type
subject
subject_type
relation
object
object_type

additional semantic roles may be included when explicitly
supported by the source text.

Possible roles include, but are not limited to:

time
location
partner
partner_with
supplier
customer
brand
market
purpose
application
condition
event_status
quantity
production_volume
capacity
through
technology
agreement
regulation
operating_segment

Additional roles are dynamic.

Use the event-specific schema to identify roles that are
particularly relevant to the current event type.

Do not add a role when its information is not supported
by the source text.


10. EVENT STATUS

When explicitly supported or clearly indicated by the source text,
event_status may be:

ongoing
planned
expected
possible
conditional
unknown

Do not use any other event_status value.

Do not assign a more certain status than the source text supports.


11. FINAL VALIDATION

Before returning each N-ary event, verify that:

1. It represents one meaningful event.

2. The event is supported by the source text.

3. The relation preserves the source action or state.

4. The subject and object are clearly identified.

5. subject_type and object_type use an allowed entity type.

6. ORG is used only for the filing company.

7. Independent actions are separated.

8. Independent objects are separated.

9. Negated statements have not been converted into positive events.

10. Hypothetical consequences have not been converted into realized events.

11. Useful semantic modifiers are preserved as additional roles.

12. event_type reflects the complete semantic meaning of the event.

13. No unsupported information has been added.
